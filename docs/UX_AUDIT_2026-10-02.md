# UX audit — 2026-10-02 (TASKS U.1)

The owner asked for a usability pass: "make the app user friendly and intuitive." This is the
audit behind it, the fixes that shipped with it (wave 1, TASKS U.1a, DECISIONS #778), and the
ranked list of what is left — including the changes that are the owner's call, not an agent's.

## How it was done

Every finding below comes from looking at the rendered app, not from reading code:

- Every signed-in route was opened on the demo dataset and screenshotted at a phone width
  (380px) and a desktop width (1280px).
- A brand-new account was created and walked through its first screens, because the demo
  never shows what a first-time reader meets.
- The screens that changed were re-checked in the real Safari engine (WebKit, iPhone 13),
  since disclosure triangles and wrapping are where Safari differs from Chrome.

Screens were judged on four questions a reader asks without saying them: *Where am I? What
is this page for? What can I tap? What does this number mean?*

## Verdict

The app is strongest where it leads with one answer: Home's cash-needed figure, the Cards
page, the Recurring and Forecast heroes, and the first-run welcome all say one thing and say
it first. It is weakest where a page explains before it shows. The same handful of patterns
accounts for most of the friction:

1. **Explanation comes before the thing it explains.** Activity opened on a screen of
   dropdowns; Inbox opened on a scorecard; Guilt-free put five paragraphs between the sum and
   the list.
2. **Things that open do not always look like they open.** Chapter triangles sat alone above
   their titles; five Coach disclosures were bold sentences with no triangle at all.
3. **Fitting a narrow column by breaking the content.** A balance split across two lines; an
   account name broken in the middle of a word.
4. **Sentences that outlived the page they described.** "Details in Recurring below" on a page
   with no Recurring section.
5. **Long pages with no way to move around them.** Settings is the longest page after the
   register and had no index.

None of these failed an existing gate. The overflow gate checks that nothing runs off the
screen; a figure broken across two lines fits perfectly. That is the case for keeping the
browser tests added in this wave: they measure what the reader sees, not what the text says.

## The rules behind the fixes

These are the idioms wave 1 applied. They are proposed as the standing rules for the rest of
the pass; the open items above are these same rules applied to the remaining pages.

1. **Every page shows its title.** On a phone the header names no page, so the title is the
   only thing saying where the reader is.
2. **Whatever opens shows that it opens**, and its closed line says what is inside. If the
   closed part qualifies a figure, the consequence stays on the closed line. A sentence that
   answers a question the reader came to the page with is not folded at all — length is not
   a reason to hide it (wave 1 tried this on Guilt-free's method paragraphs and was sent back).
3. **Content is never broken to fit a column.** A figure stays on one line; a name wraps at a
   space. If it does not fit, the layout changes, not the content.
4. **A pointer is a link to a page — the page where the thing is named.** "Above" and "below"
   are allowed only where a test pins the order they describe.
5. **The page's job comes first.** What the reader came to do or see leads; method, caveats
   and scorecards follow and fold.
6. **A long page has an index.**
7. **Dates read the way a person writes them.**

## Proposals that need the owner

**U.1b — Activity rows on a phone.** Keep, always visible: payee, amount, category, date,
account, and one status chip at most (Pending, or "AI guess — needs your OK"). Move Details
and Rule… into the row's ⋯ menu on phones only, where every other row action already lives;
the payee stays the tap target for the detail view. Fixed/Discretionary and Known merchant
become one quiet line under the category rather than two bordered chips. Desktop keeps the
current row. This roughly halves row height on a phone. It needs a yes because "Rule… on the
row" and "Details on the row" were each asked for by name.

**U.1c — Phone navigation.** Swap Calendar for Activity in the five tabs (Calendar moves to
More, and stays one tap from Home's "See it on the calendar" link), and move Sign out from
the header into More and Settings. This changes where things are for a reader who has
learned the current layout, which is why it is not done here.

## What was deliberately left alone

- **Ask Aimplifi.** Another slice (#777) is in flight on that page.
- **Any figure, or the sentence that states one.** The fold in finding 8 moves the three
  limits behind a tap; it does not change a word of them. The one new sentence (their closed
  summary) says such cases exist, how many are listed inside, and the clause all three
  already end on — it does not restate the rules, because two attempts to compress them
  were each wrong.
- **Desktop layouts**, except where a fix was the same component (chapter headers, chevrons,
  page titles).
