## #782 — A combined-away account is not listed in "Net worth today" (2026-10-03)

**Context.** Owner: "some of my accounts are being counted twice, specifically 0977." Measured on production, read-only and owner-authorized in-session (`scripts/audit-probes/dup-0977-*.mts`; live figures stay out of the repo by owner choice): the two `CREDIT CARD ····0977` rows were already combined by an active, user-confirmed reconciliation link, and replaying the shipped boundary + engines over the live rows counted the card ONCE in net worth, in cash-needed, in every recorded trend point, and in transactions. What the reader saw was the LIVE point: `netWorthSeries` built "Net worth today" from every account in the boundary output, so every combined-away row on the account (the old 0977 among them) sat in that list at $0.00 — the card twice, by name. The same rows made `netWorthDelta` see accounts "join" since the previous point, so the change line refused: "No comparison — N accounts joined since …".

**Decision.** `netWorthSeries` takes a REQUIRED `supersededAccountIds` (the boundary's effective predecessors) and leaves those rows out of the live point only, and only at a $0.00 balance. They stay in `accounts` so their pre-cutover snapshots still resolve into history. Both callers (`getDashboardData`, `getAccountsView`) pass the boundary's own list. Required, like `accountType`, so a direct caller cannot drop it with tsc clean — though the dashboard reads it from `FinanceSnapshot.supersededAccountIds`, which is optional (`?? []`, the same pattern as `resolvePaymentAccount`), so a provider that omitted it would fail open to the old cosmetic $0.00 rows, never to a wrong figure.

**Why zero-only.** The boundary zeroes every effective predecessor, so the filter removes only lines that add nothing; a superseded row that somehow still carried a balance is in the headline and stays in the live point, so Σ constituents === headline holds by construction. Measured on the owner's data: the live list lost exactly its combined-away rows, the net-worth figure was identical to the cent, and the change line printed a dollar change again.

**Alternatives.** Drop predecessors from `accounts` (loses their pre-cutover history — the snapshot lookup skips unknown accounts). Filter in the drilldown component (the delta engine reads the same constituents, so the change line would still refuse). Delete the old rows (destroys the history the combine exists to keep).

**Found, not changed.** The snapshot writer still records balance rows for disconnected predecessors; the boundary drops them, no figure moves. Some older cross-provider (SimpleFIN→Plaid) links on the owner's account pair rows whose names name different accounts; balances are unaffected (predecessors zeroed) but those rows' pre-cutover history is attributed to the linked account — the U.15 re-audit's territory.

**Critic (Opus, separate context, cycle 1): PASS — 0 P0 / 0 P1 / 6 P2.** Taken: a $0.00 account that was not combined stays in the live point (P2-2, the critic's surviving mutant now dies); the series doc comment (P2-4); this entry's "required" wording (P2-5); the production probes abort unless exactly one user owns a 0977 row and read the pair from the link rather than hardcoded ids (P2-6). Recorded, not taken: the drilldown's U.6 "counted here as money you owned/owed" label reads current class from the live point, so a combined-away row's pre-cutover history rows lose it when their recorded class differs from today's (P2-1; no figure moves); eight reconcile-boundary / two snapshot-plan tests compose boundary → series with `supersededAccountIds: []` rather than the boundary's list (P2-3; their figure assertions still hold).

**Locked.** `tests/unit/networth-series.test.ts` "combined-away rows leave the live point" (6 tests, invented balances; FAIL-OLD 2 failed on the prior engine); e2e `tests/e2e/combined-live-point.spec.ts`.

## #781 — Ask compares two months, and averages a run of them, on the accounts whose records cover them (2026-10-03)

*(Scope note, after critic cycle 5: every effective combined card is "trimmed" — the boundary always drops a predecessor's days after its cutover — so the reader's words, the questions and an older record's feed (points 3–4 below) apply to accounts that stand alone; a combined card is answered from the days its records keep plus its current record's own feed, or left out by name.)*

**Context.** Ask answered "how much did I spend in May?" but not "did I spend more in May than April?" — of 23 ordinary follow-up questions measured for #777, production answered 18 with ONE period's figure under a question about two. #777 tried to read every English phrasing and to infer from the pattern of rows whether a quiet account was closed or broken; eight hostile-critic cycles failed, each on a new phrasing or a new household the inference got wrong. That work is parked, unmerged, on the local branch `ask-analyst-777-parked`. This slice is the narrow rebuild the owner then directed to finish and ship (2026-10-02: "Finish Ask … get an independent review, and ship").

**Decision.**
1. **A closed grammar** (`engine/assistant/analyst-grammar.ts`): "compare [X] spending in MONTH to MONTH₂", "did I spend more/less [on X] in MONTH than MONTH₂", "[X] spending in MONTH vs MONTH₂", "average monthly [X] spending [over the last N months]". Every word of a sentence must be accounted for; anything else returns null and goes exactly where production sent it. The LLM path cannot mint these intents.
2. **The subject is read exactly as the one-month question reads it** — "how much did I spend on ‹phrase› last month" — and only when the phrase IS that subject (a synonym owning the whole phrase whose target is that subject, the subject's own name, or the reader's category of that name being that subject), and only a spending subject (never Income, Transfer, Credit Card Payment, Investment & Savings, Loan Payment, nor a group holding one). So the comparison and the one-month figure never name the same words as different things, and "non-grocery", "medical bills", "costco groceries" are not read as categories.
3. **Facts, never inference** (`engine/analyst/same-account.ts`): an account is IN a comparison only if its record is known whole for each period — a row on or before the start, and a row on or after the end, a live feed that succeeded past it, or the reader's own word. Everything else that ever spent is LEFT OUT of both sides (never $0), named with what it has on record and why, and — where a yes/no can settle it and the answer could change the figure — asked once ("Is Apr 10, 2026 the last time Old Card was used?" — worded so a card still in use can answer it truthfully). The answer is kept on the Account row against the edge date it was given for (four nullable columns; void once the record grows past that date), audit-logged, and the question re-answered in place.
4. **Two records of one card are not one whole record by being joined.** Where a combined card's records do not meet, the days between are a stated gap; the card is left out of any period the gap touches until the new record is said to have begun there AND the old one is known complete to it (its feed or the reader's word). Each record covers its own stored row span (capped at today) MINUS the date ranges the reconciliation boundary drops for it — `reconciliationDroppedRanges`, exported by the boundary module as the exact complement of its keep rule (a property test checks them day by day). A gap is a day no kept record covers; it is provable only between a record's OWN last row and another's OWN first row; and a day a record has rows for that the boundary dropped and no other record keeps is activity counted nowhere — the card stays out and nothing is asked, since no answer can bring dropped rows back.
5. **The figure is the app's own** (`spendingByCategory` over the included rows taken together — never a sum of per-account figures), so a month in a comparison is the number Ask gives for that month whenever every account is in. When any account is left out the headline itself says "On the N accounts with complete records, …".
6. **A month in progress is compared day for day**: its finished days (through yesterday) against the same days of the other month, said so in the answer; on the 1st, it is refused by name.
7. **Follow-up buttons**: a one-month answer's third chip is the comparison; a comparison offers the same month last year and a six-month average. A button is offered only if its own text — read with the reader's own category words, as the tap will be — reads back to exactly its intent AND the app would answer it rather than refuse; on the 1st, a comparison of the month just begun offers the two finished months behind it.

**Alternatives considered.** Keep #777's open-English route (eight failed cycles; the bar is unreachable over open English). Infer closure from row patterns (undecidable: a closed card and a broken feed look the same). Answer with one period's figure (what production does; the question is about two).

**Critic.** Cycle 1 (two lanes, Opus, separate contexts): money FAIL 1 P0 / 1 P1 / 6 P2 — P0: a combined card with a hole between its records was priced at $0 ("$0.00 in July — 100% lower"); P1: the headline read as the reader's total with accounts left out. Routing FAIL 0 P0 / 6 P1 / 7 P2 — the subject slot read a category word anywhere in a phrase; non-spending categories answered "No spending"; three wordings answered worse than production (the roster's "subscriptions", "vacation" read two ways on one screen, "this month vs last month" refused); a chip led back to its own refusal; a stale tap showed Next.js's raw production error; driver labels were truncated. All fixed and locked (below); P2s taken: future-dated rows no longer prove an edge, the lineage's feed comes from the account that carries it now, the average says "the last N months" only while it is, a failing-feed edge cannot be overridden by a direct call, the write is audit-logged, "on the 1 account", no question that cannot change the figure, questions only about named accounts, an unyeared second month after a yeared one, focus moves to the new headline, prompts grouped with their buttons. Found by the maker while adding the spec to the WebKit project (present on main): text typed before hydration left Ask disabled — the view now adopts the box's text on mount. Recorded, not taken: a Plaid sync that succeeds at the API is taken as fresh bank data; a hand-kept predecessor marks a combined card "kept by hand"; the left-out sentence can run about 12 lines at 380px; two taps in one frame both fire (idempotent). Cycle 2 (two fresh lanes): money FAIL 0 P0 / 1 P1 / 7 P2 — every cycle-1 money finding confirmed fixed; P1: a cutover earlier than the old record's last row dropped its later rows while the raw spans still met, so the dropped days were priced at $0 ("$300.00 in July — 50% lower", truth $600). Routing FAIL 0 P0 / 2 P1 / 8 P2 — all six cycle-1 P1s confirmed fixed (0 one-month-vs-comparison reading differences across every built-in name, down from 74); N1: the whole-phrase proofs were not tied to the subject chosen, so a reader's "Dog Food" was compared as Food & Dining ("$9,300.00 … 794% higher"); N2: chips were read back without the reader's own words, and a "last month" chip under a group answer named a different subject. All fixed and locked (`assistant-analyst-boundary.test.ts` runs the real boundary). P2s taken: a tie on the latest row goes to the account that carries the card now; a refund-only account is not "nothing on record" and is asked; one-day wording ("on the first day of", "same day", "both periods"); a small-sample note under seven days; "your answer is saved" when only the refresh fails; non-spending groups declined; the nearest month of a name, ties to the later; a 48-character cap before the nested parse; the end question reworded. Fixed by taking those days from the boundary itself (above) and treating dropped-but-uncovered days as lost activity; P2s taken: a cutover before the first row now keeps everything, as the boundary does; no word is accepted about a dropped edge; the dropped-gap sentence says why ("…stopped counting when it was combined with another account"); only rows the figure can count make a question worth asking; a failing audit write no longer reads as "could not save". ## #780 — SimpleFIN is retired as a way to connect; everything already connected through it stays (2026-10-02)

*(#779 is held by the compact register row, built on branch `uiux-pass` and waiting at the human gate — not on `main`. This entry takes the next number so that slice does not have to renumber.)*

**Context.** SimpleFIN was the bridge to live bank data while Plaid production access was pending. Plaid is live. The owner, 2026-10-02: "Simplefin was just a bridge until we can get plaid. We have plaid now. I don't mind getting rid of simplefin if we don't break stuff." Checked against production first (read-only): no `SimpleFinConnection` row exists; 12 Plaid items synced that day with no error; 24 `provider = 'simplefin'` accounts remain, every one joined to a Plaid successor by a confirmed reconciliation; 1,357 transactions and 57 holdings came in through SimpleFIN. So nothing depends on a SimpleFIN connection today — but months of stored history do depend on SimpleFIN ROWS.

**Decision.** Retire it as a way IN, and only that.
1. **Nothing offers a new SimpleFIN connection.** The "+ Connect a bank (SimpleFIN)" door and its setup-token form are gone from the Accounts page and from the first-run panel (which every empty page reuses). A reader who never used SimpleFIN now sees no trace of it; the Settings connections card no longer names it. The owner's own Accounts page was showing that first-time door, because all 24 of his SimpleFIN accounts are superseded.
2. **An existing connection is untouched.** Its panel on Accounts (freshness, history depth, Sync now, Disconnect), the sync code, the auto-sync, and the dashboard alert that repairs a failing connection (including pasting a fresh token) all stay. `connectSimplefin` stays as a server action because that repair path calls it.
3. **Accounts that outlived their connection are still told so** (K.2b). The notice keeps its facts — the connection is gone, how many accounts, when the data stopped, that saved transactions are kept — and instead of "Reconnect below" says SimpleFIN is no longer offered and points at the Plaid button directly under it.
4. **No stored row, column, table or read path changes.** No schema change, no migration, no data script. The privacy policy still names SimpleFIN: it describes how a SimpleFIN access URL is stored and who the data is shared with, which stays true for any connection that exists.

**Not done, deliberately.** Deleting the provider module, the sync engine, the `SimpleFinConnection` table or the `provider = 'simplefin'` branches across forty-odd engine files. Each of those reads stored rows; removing them is the "break stuff" the owner asked to avoid, for no reader-visible gain. Two historical live-check scripts (`h5-`, `k2b-live-deploy-check.mjs`) still look for the removed button; they describe past deploys and are left as written.

**A consequence to know.** In an environment with no Plaid credentials, the only ways to get data in are now CSV import and manual accounts. Demo mode is unaffected (rule 4): it never used either provider.

**Critic (separate context, Sonnet): PASS — zero P0/P1.** It seeded every state on its own server — a live connection, stranded accounts, partly and wholly superseded accounts, a failing connection — and found no remaining offer anywhere in the app except the privacy policy. Its P2s, taken: Disconnect became one-way without saying so (the connected panel now says it is final, before it is pressed); the privacy policy named SimpleFIN as a current way to connect (it now names it only for connections made before the retirement; last-updated 2026-10-02). Recorded, not taken: the stranded-accounts notice stays up after the bank is re-linked through Plaid until the old rows are combined or deleted (unverified on screen — no Plaid credentials in the test environment); `docs/SIMPLEFIN_WALKTHROUGH.md` is still a connect walkthrough, linked from nothing.

**Locked.** `tests/unit/simplefin-retired.test.ts` — both halves: nothing offers it; what an existing reader depends on is still there. `tests/e2e/connection-health.spec.ts` (a reader who never used it sees no trace on Accounts and is offered Plaid; stranded accounts get the notice, no SimpleFIN door, and the Plaid button below it), `guided-onboarding.spec.ts` (first-run offers Plaid, CSV and manual, and never SimpleFIN), `transactions.spec.ts`.

## #779 — The compact register row: two lines until it is opened, every control one tap away (2026-10-02)

**Context.** The owner sent a screenshot of Activity on his own account, taken on his iPhone hours after #778 shipped: two full rows and the top of a third filled the screen. A real-account row carries about eighteen controls and labels — Filter, Pending, Details, Bank text, Rule…, the class, the provenance, a suggestion and its Confirm, category, date, account, Note, Tax tag, Exclude, Reimburse, Tag, the amount, a Money in/out flip and ⋯ — most of them 44px tap targets, wrapped into the ~175px left of the amount. Measured on a signed-up test account: **300–350px a row, two rows per screen.** #778's audit ranked this twelfth because it only ever opened the demo, which renders none of the edit controls. Every one of those controls was asked for by name as something to have *on the row*, so the choice was put to the owner with three mock-ups; he chose "compact, opens in place".

**Decision.**
1. **Below `sm`, a row is closed until its chevron opens it.** Closed, it shows what a reader scans for and what says something is waiting: payee, amount, category, date, account, the Fixed/Discretionary label (the page lead promises one on every row), and the chips that are facts or asks — Pending, the handover marker, Excluded from totals, Awaiting reimbursement / Reimbursed, tags, the note/tax control when the row has a note or a tax class (it reads the tax class, or the one word "Note" — the closed row says a note exists, not what it says; the text is one tap in), the one provenance badge that needs an OK ("AI guess") with its Confirm, and a suggestion with its Confirm. A badge that only reports how the category was reached waits for the open — including "Needs a category", because the category chip under it already reads "Uncategorized". Open, it shows every control it had. From `sm` up nothing changes and there is no chevron.
1a. **On a phone the row is a grid, not "controls beside the amount".** Chevron in its own narrow column at the left (first in the DOM, so Tab from it lands in the row it opened); payee and amount on the first line; chips, the category line, and — open — the Money in/out flip and ⋯ on lines that span the full content width. The desktop wrappers go `display: contents` below `sm` so one DOM serves both layouts. The two pickers open from the row's left edge so a 288px panel fits a 360px phone.
2. **Hidden with CSS, not unmounted.** One class (`ROW_REST`) on each control that waits for the open; a `data-open` attribute on the row. Every control stays mounted with its state and test id, desktop renders the row it always did, and nothing about a control depends on whether its row is open.
3. **One Set for the list**, like the three menu controllers already there — no hook per row. Whether a row is open is ONLY that Set (plus the harness's all-open flag). The one panel that can be opened from a closed row — note/tax, on a row that has either — puts the row in the Set as it opens, so the row stays open after the panel is dismissed; and the chevron closes a row's panels with it. (The first two cuts derived "open" from the panel being up; see cycle 2.)
4. **Open rows survive the reload every edit ends in** (`register-open-rows.ts`, sessionStorage — a place in a task, not a preference). They are restored in a LAYOUT effect that writes `data-open` on the DOM itself before telling React: `RegisterScrollRestorer` puts the reader back at an offset that was measured with those rows open, in a passive effect that runs before a state-only restore would re-render. Reopening after the scroll moves the reader's place by every reopened row above it, and Safari has no scroll anchoring to absorb that.
5. **The e2e suite runs with every row open** (`__AIMPLIFI_E2E_OPEN_ROWS`, the Home/Coach chapter precedent): those specs drive controls that now sit behind the chevron. `tests/e2e/activity-row.spec.ts` sets `KEEP_ROWS_CLOSED` and locks what production renders, on a signed-up account with seeded rows.

**Measured after** (same fixture, eight rows, the Playwright touch profiles — Pixel 5 at 380px and iPhone 13 at 390px): closed rows 85–129px, mean 103px — **about 7½ rows on an 800px screen, 6½ on an iPhone 13's 664px** (was two). Those closed heights belong to this fixture: a row carrying two tags, a reimbursement chip and a note closes at about 155px (critic, cycle 2). Opened, against the same eight rows at the commit before (`ca40f713`), measured the same day:

| Width | Opened row, after | The same row, before |
|---|---|---|
| 360px | 226–292px; the long-note row 336px | 302–399px; the long-note row 376px |
| 380px (Chromium) | 226–267px; long-note 320px | 302–353px; long-note 328px |
