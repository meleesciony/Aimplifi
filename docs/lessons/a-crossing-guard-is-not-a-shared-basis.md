# A crossing guard is not a shared basis

**Summary:** detecting that two figures can cross, and refusing the subtraction when they do, is not making them the same question. The safe clamp hides the months where one side is still exact.

## How it bit (C.20)

`spentSoFarCents` is the sum of surviving category nets — a net-refunded category drops to zero. The pace rate credit was a per-merchant sum of raw rows. #391 saw they could cross and took no credit (over-project, the safe direction). The P1-2 fixture — one dropped-category bill plus $30 of dining — produced the same $30 rate either way, so the lock pinned the guard rather than a shared basis.

A second admitted bill in a healthy category exposes the gap: shopping nets to zero, electricity $80 posts in full, dining $30. The merchant raw credit is $100+$80 against a $110 total → the guard takes no credit → the $80 stays in the daily rate and the month projects $330.00 instead of $170.00.

## The rule

When two figures are subtracted, they must be derived from the same drop, not merely prevented from crossing. Share the surviving array (here, `spendingByCategory`'s `byCategory`) so the credit cannot name money the total already discarded. A last-resort crossing guard may stay; it is not the fix.

Still-due is a different question ("did the charge land?") and must not be coupled to the reduced credit: a posted bill whose category was netted out is not demanded again.
